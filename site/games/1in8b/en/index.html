How your birthplace is drawn
The first picks a place by its number of births each year, the second by its population. Either way, life starts at age 0.
Length of one life
The time it takes to live to the local life expectancy. At 1 hour, a year passes in about 40–60 seconds. You can pause or speed up while you live.

    How it works

    Born in the same second

    In the same second you are born, other babies are born all over the world. Four of them live out their lives by the same statistics, shown alongside yours. No one chose where to be born.

    A pause

    At 5, 15, 30, 50 and 70, time stops and asks you one question. Think about this person’s life and your own together, and leave a line if you like. At the end, you can say a few words to this person.

    What comes from real data

    For 172 countries and territories: births, life expectancy by sex, infant and under-5 mortality, GDP per person (PPP), income inequality, years of schooling, university enrollment, employment in agriculture, fertility rate, maternal mortality, HIV, homicide rate, smoking rate, out-of-pocket health costs, child marriage rate and life satisfaction. The age at death comes from the life tables of each country and territory by sex, and causes of death follow the patterns of the WHO Global Health Estimates by region and age.

    If making decisions feels like too much, start a life and turn on 'Auto mode' in the bar at the top (the ⋯ menu on phones) to simply watch. You pick a focus for each year and make choices when decisions come up about school, work, love, moving abroad or medical care. Choices change a life, but it is hard to fully escape the statistics of the place you were born. Today’s world is calculated with today’s statistics, a past world with that year’s, and the coming world with UN and other projections. Income comes from the country’s income distribution (based on the Gini index), and amounts are shown in purchasing-power (PPP) dollars and in US terms, as they would feel at US prices. Suicide also happens from the teenage years on, as often as the real statistics say, without showing how or why.

    Death, illness, war, poverty, drinking and gambling, drug dependence, violence at home, sexual violence and suicide happen here as often as the real statistics say, never shown in detail and always with where to find help, and a past world tells of real history such as wars and mass killings. It is made for ages 15 and up.

    What do these mean?
    • Health Affects the risk of dying and whether you can keep working. Exercise, checkups and looking after yourself raise it; age lowers it.
    • Happiness How satisfied you are with life. Income, family and friends, health and debt all affect it.
    • Knowledge Affects entrance exams, job offers and your starting pay. Your family's means and the schools where you live matter more, and above about 70 more of it makes little difference.
    • Relationships Your bonds with family and friends. They affect marriage and its crises, talks with your children, the chance of being bullied at school, and loneliness in old age.

    Higher numbers raise the odds; they never decide the outcome. As in real life, luck and circumstances play their part too.
